# Comparison over Estimation

## Statement

ORDR values comparison over estimation.

When information is incomplete, relative judgment is more reliable than absolute measurement.

## Rationale

People are generally better at comparing things than estimating them.

Questions such as:

- Which opportunity is more valuable?
- Which option is more uncertain?
- Which initiative is more complex?

are often easier to answer than:

- How valuable is it?
- How uncertain is it?
- How complex is it?

Estimations frequently create an illusion of precision without increasing understanding.

ORDR therefore prefers relative positioning, ranking, and trade-off analysis over scores, percentages, points, and other forms of artificial precision.

## Implications

- Opportunities are compared rather than scored.
- Complexity is ordered rather than estimated.
- Uncertainty is ranked rather than quantified.
- Priorities emerge from comparison rather than calculation.
- Precision is avoided unless supported by evidence.

The objective is not to produce accurate numbers.

The objective is to make better decisions.

## Questions

When evaluating opportunities:

- Which is more valuable?
- Which is more uncertain?
- Which is more urgent?
- Which creates more future options?
- Which would I investigate first?

These questions often produce more reliable answers than attempting to assign exact measurements.

## Guiding Thought

> Estimation attempts to measure the unknown.
>
> Comparison reveals meaningful differences.
>
> When certainty is limited, ordering is often more trustworthy than measurement.
