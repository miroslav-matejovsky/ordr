# Knowledge over Activity

## Statement

ORDR values knowledge creation over activity completion.

Progress is measured by improved understanding, not by time spent, documents produced, or tasks completed.

## Rationale

Activity is easy to observe.

Research can be performed.
Meetings can be attended.
Documents can be written.
Notes can be collected.

None of these activities guarantee better decisions.

Knowledge emerges only when uncertainty is reduced and understanding improves.

ORDR therefore measures progress through learning rather than effort.

## Implications

- Validated assumptions are progress.
- Invalidated assumptions are progress.
- Eliminated options are progress.
- Newly discovered constraints are progress.
- Refined understanding is progress.

The amount of work performed is secondary.

The change in understanding is primary.

## Questions

When reviewing an opportunity:

- What do we know now that we did not know before?
- What uncertainty was reduced?
- Which assumptions were validated?
- Which assumptions were disproved?
- Which options became more or less viable?

If understanding has not improved, progress has not occurred.

## Guiding Thought

> Activity consumes resources.
>
> Knowledge creates options.
>
> ORDR exists to increase understanding, not to document effort.
