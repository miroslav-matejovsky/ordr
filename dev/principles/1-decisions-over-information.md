# Decisions over Information

## Statement

ORDR exists to improve decisions, not to collect information.

Information has no intrinsic value within ORDR. Its value emerges only when it helps clarify, challenge, or support a decision.

## Rationale

The easiest way to create the illusion of progress is to accumulate notes, documents, research, links, maps, and analyses.

However, information alone does not create outcomes.

A smaller body of information that enables a clear decision is more valuable than a large body of information that does not.

ORDR therefore treats information as a means rather than an end.

## Implications

- Every record should contribute to a current or future decision.
- Research without a decision context is noise.
- Evidence is collected to reduce decision uncertainty.
- Reviews focus on decisions becoming clearer, not repositories becoming larger.
- The quality of a system is measured by the quality of decisions it enables.

## Questions

When adding information to ORDR:

- What decision does this support?
- What uncertainty does this reduce?
- What option does this strengthen or weaken?
- What action becomes possible because of this knowledge?

If none of these questions can be answered, the information likely does not belong in ORDR.

## Guiding Thought

> Information is stored only to improve judgment.
>
> The ultimate output of ORDR is not knowledge.
>
> It is better decisions.
